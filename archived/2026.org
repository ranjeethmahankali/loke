** DONE Adaptive sampling
   CLOSED: [2026-10-20 Tue 17:21]

   - State "NEXT"       from "TODO"       [2026-10-20 Tue 16:42]

   Equal steps in the parameter angle =φ=, sized for the worst point on the ellipse.

   1. Error of one span. For points at =φ= and =φ + Δ=, the sum-to-product identities give =(P(φ) +
      P(φ+Δ))/2 − c = cos(Δ/2) · (P(φ + Δ/2) − c)=.  So the gap between the chord's midpoint and the
      curve's midpoint is =(1 − cos(Δ/2))·(P_mid − c)=, whose length is at most =(1 − cos(Δ/2))·A=.
   2. That gap bounds the distance from every point of the span to the chord *segment*, provided =Δ
      <= π=. On a circle, an arc of at most a half turn lies inside the rectangle with sides "chord"
      and "sagitta". The ellipse is an affine image of the circle, and affine maps send that
      rectangle to a parallelogram with sides "chord" and =g = (1 − cos(Δ/2))·(P_mid − c)=.  Every
      curve point is a point of the chord plus at most =g=, so it is within =|g| <= (1 −
      cos(Δ/2))·A= of the segment. The bound is tight near the ends of the major axis.
   3. Step: =Δ = 4·asin(sqrt(tol/(2A)))=, which equals =2·acos(1 − tol/A)= (=1 − cos x = 2
      sin²(x/2)=) but keeps full relative precision for small =tol/A=, where =1 − tol/A= rounds away
      the low bits of =tol/A= (in f32, =Δ= is off by several percent at =tol/A = 1e-6=, and is 0
      below about =6e-8=). Add =asin= to =TrigonometryAdaptor=, and switch =Arc::adaptive_samples=
      to the same form so the two stay bit-identical.  Clamping =tol/A= to 1 gives =Δ = π=,
      so 2. applies. Then =n = max(1, ceil(θ/Δ))= and use =Δ = θ/n= so the steps are equal. Emit
      =n + 1= points. For a circle (=A = r=) the step is exactly =Arc::adaptive_samples='s. If =Δ=
      underflows to 0 (=tol/2A= below the smallest float), use the 0.1 rad fallback.
   4. Evaluation without per-point trig. Compute =(cos Δ, sin Δ)= once with =sin_cos=. Advance =(cos
      φ, sin φ)= by rotating it with that pair (4 multiplies, 2 adds), and emit =c + cos φ·U + sin
      φ·V=.
   5. Drift. The rotation recurrence accumulates about =k·ε= error after =k= steps, which is visible
      in f32 (about 1e-4 relative at 1000 points).  Re-seed =(cos φ, sin φ)= exactly with
      =sin_cos(k·Δ)= every 32 steps.
   6. Tests:
      - For every pair of consecutive samples, densely sampled curve points between them are within
        =tolerance= of the chord segment (clamped projection), over a variety of tilted, thin (A:B
        up to 100:1), major, semicircle, near-full-turn (=θ= close to 2π, built with =flip_dir=) and
        projected arcs.
      - The point count is =ceil(θ/Δ) + 1=.
      - For circular arcs (=EllipticArc::from(&arc)=), the step angle equals =Arc='s and the point
        count is within one of =Arc::adaptive_samples= (=Arc= splits the sweep into two halves,
        =2·ceil(θ/2Δ) + 1= points).
      - First and last points equal =start()= and =end()= exactly.
      - An =EllipticArc3f= with a tight tolerance (1000+ points) stays within a small multiple of
        f32 ε of =point(t)= at every sample.
      - =tolerance <= 0= and =tolerance >= A= behave as described above.
      - A tiny f32 tolerance (=tol/A= around 1e-9) still yields a finite, correct sample count.

   Known trade-off, not to be done now: equal steps use more points than needed on the flat parts of
   thin ellipses. A curvature-driven step needs about 82% of the points at A:B = 2:1, 53% at 10:1
   and 17% at 100:1, but costs a =sin_cos= and two =sqrt=s per point. If thin ellipses turn out to
   be common, split the sweep into a few pieces, each with its own equal step sized for the worst
   point inside it; that keeps the cheap recurrence.

** DONE Uniform sampling
   CLOSED: [2026-10-20 Tue 18:05]
   :LOGBOOK:
   - State "DONE"       from "NEXT"       [2026-10-20 Tue 18:05]
   - State "NEXT"       from              [2026-10-20 Tue 17:21]
   :END:

   There is no closed form from arc length back to =φ=, and inverting the Carlson length per point
   is far too slow. Follow =Spline::uniform_samples= (in =src/spline.rs=: walk adaptive samples,
   accumulate span lengths, interpolate within spans), specialized for the ellipse.

   1. Walk the adaptive =φ= grid from the section above, using the same =tolerance=, rotation
      recurrence and re-seeding, with the step also capped at 1/16 rad.
   2. Length of each span: Simpson's rule on the speed (start, middle, end), 2 =sqrt=s per span
      since neighbouring spans share the end speed. Chord-based estimates like =Spline='s are
      always short, so their errors add up along the arc (several =tol= on thin ellipses);
      Simpson's errors cancel. 2-point Gauss–Legendre is as accurate, but its nodes are not at
      the span ends, and the end speeds are needed for 3.
   3. Place the outputs with a cubic Hermite inverse =φ(s)=: it matches =φ= and =dφ/ds = 1/v= at
      both span ends, written as a cubic in the distance into the span. Linear interpolation is
      off by about =tol=; this by a few thousandths of =tol= on moderate ellipses, and stays below
      =tol= at A:B = 100:1. Slopes are clamped (Fritsch–Carlson) to keep it monotone and finite
      where the speed is zero. For circles it is exact.
   4. Evaluate each point without trig: =δ <= 1/16=, so short Taylor series for =sin δ= (to δ⁹)
      and =cos δ= (to δ⁸) are exact to f64 rounding, then rotate the span's frame.
   5. Speed: the next span's frame, length and speeds are set up one span ahead so their =sqrt=s
      overlap with the current span's outputs; the Hermite coefficients are only computed for
      spans an output lands in; one division gives 1/L and both 1/v.
   6. Stop when the target distance passes the end of the arc. Like =Arc= and =Spline=, do not
      force-emit the end point. Edge cases: =step <= 0= yields nothing, =start < 0= is clamped to
      0, a degenerate sweep yields =start()= when =start= is 0 and nothing otherwise.
   7. Tests:
      - Distances between consecutive samples, measured along the curve with the exact Carlson
        length of the sub-arc (recovering each sample's =φ= from a 2×2 solve), equal =step=
        within 0.01 =tol= (0.2 at 10:1, 1.0 at 100:1), on tilted, thin, major, near-full-turn and
        projected arcs, and the sample count is =floor((L − start)/step) + 1=.
      - The span cap (huge =tol=) and the fallback (=tol <= 0=) stay accurate.
      - The first sample is exactly =start()=, also for negative =start=.
      - Circular arcs match =Arc::uniform_samples= to 1e-12 relative, including at the cap.
      - A flat ellipse (zero speed at the start), edge cases, and f32 against f64.
   8. Benches =elliptic_arc_uniform_samples_dense= (4 samples per span) and =_sparse= (one per 10
      spans).

   Follow-up, separate task: =Arc::adaptive_samples= and =Arc::uniform_samples= call =sin= twice per
   point; the same rotation recurrence would speed them up.
